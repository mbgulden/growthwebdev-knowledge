# Cognitive Bias and Fallacy Index

This index aggregates, categorizes, and deconstructs the psychological mechanisms that sustain entrenched, maladaptive belief systems.

> [!NOTE]
> Understanding the cognitive barriers to change is the first step in designing non-coercive intervention vectors.

---

## 1. Deconstructed Cognitive Biases

### A. Confirmation Bias
*   **Operational Definition**: The tendency to search for, interpret, favor, and recall information in a way that confirms one's prior beliefs or values.
*   **Psychological Origins**: Evolutionary pressure to minimize cognitive effort and preserve psychological safety within tribal/social structures.
*   **Mechanism in Belief Formation**: Acts as an information filter, systematically discarding contradictory data while amplifying supporting anecdotes.
*   **Real-World Example**: A subject in an online conspiracy echo chamber who registers only the failed predictions of mainstream media while ignoring similar or worse failures in alternative media.
*   **Methodological Countermeasure**: [Socratic Questioning](./methodologies/socratic_questioning.md), specifically highlighting information processing inconsistencies.
*   **Detailed Node**: [Confirmation Bias](./cognitive_biases/confirmation_bias.md) (exploring its core operational definitions).

### B. The Backfire Effect
*   **Operational Definition**: A cognitive phenomenon where presenting corrective facts actually increases the subject's belief in the original misconception.
*   **Psychological Origins**: Identity protection. When a core belief is challenged, the brain perceives it as a threat to personal survival.
*   **Mechanism in Belief Formation**: Challenging data triggers flight-or-fight mechanisms, leading to defensive rationalization and doubling down on the belief.
*   **Real-World Example**: Showing scientific consensus reports to a vaccine skeptic, who then concludes the scientific community is part of a coordinated cover-up.
*   **Methodological Countermeasure**: [Street Epistemology](./methodologies/street_epistemology.md), prioritizing epistemological foundation analysis over factual debate.
*   **Detailed Node**: [The Backfire Effect](./cognitive_biases/backfire_effect.md) (exploring identity-defense reactions).

### C. Cognitive Dissonance
*   **Operational Definition**: The mental discomfort experienced when holding two or more contradictory beliefs, ideas, or values.
*   **Psychological Origins**: The human drive for internal consistency. Inconsistencies cause psychological alarm.
*   **Mechanism in Belief Formation**: Leads to defensive cognitive adaptations (like rationalization or avoidance) to escape the discomfort.
*   **Real-World Example**: A health enthusiast who follows a highly restrictive protocol but experiences declining health, rationalizing the deterioration as "detoxification."
*   **Methodological Countermeasure**: [Cognitive Behavioral Restructuring](./methodologies/cbt_restructuring.md) (assisting in mapping and resolving contradictions).
*   **Detailed Node**: [Cognitive Dissonance](./cognitive_biases/cognitive_dissonance.md) (mapping internal friction and resolution paths).

### D. Authority Bias
*   **Operational Definition**: The tendency to attribute greater accuracy to the opinion of an authority figure and be more influenced by that opinion.
*   **Psychological Origins**: Social conditioning and survival advantage in trusting parental and community leadership.
*   **Mechanism in Belief Formation**: Anchors belief validity to the status of a guru or group leader rather than objective evidence.
*   **Real-World Example**: Adopting the "Bio-Prism Protocol" solely because its founder is framed as an enlightened medical intuitive.
*   **Methodological Countermeasure**: [Socratic Questioning](./methodologies/socratic_questioning.md) (deconstructing authority-based compliance).
*   **Detailed Node**: [Authority Bias](./cognitive_biases/authority_bias.md) (evaluating authority-based compliance).

### E. Tribalism Bias (In-group Bias)
*   **Operational Definition**: The tendency to conform to group beliefs and reject outside information to maintain belonging and protect social standing.
*   **Psychological Origins**: Social cohesion and evolutionary reliance on the tribe for physical safety.
*   **Mechanism in Belief Formation**: Establishes strict in-group boundaries, treating outside opinions or consensus as hostile attacks.
*   **Real-World Example**: Conforming to extreme lifestyle protocols to maintain membership and respect in a digital community.
*   **Methodological Countermeasure**: [Street Epistemology](./methodologies/street_epistemology.md) and non-confrontational dialogue.
*   **Detailed Node**: [Tribalism Bias](./cognitive_biases/tribalism_bias.md) (deconstructing echo chamber dynamics).
