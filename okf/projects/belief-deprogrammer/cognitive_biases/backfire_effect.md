# The Backfire Effect

The Backfire Effect is a highly defensive cognitive bias that presents a major barrier to logical intervention.

## Operational Definition
When a person's core beliefs are challenged with contradictory evidence, they experience this challenge as a personal threat, causing them to defend and double down on their original belief rather than revise it.

## Psychological Origins
Identity protection. The brain processes challenges to core worldviews using the same neural pathways that process physical threats (flight-or-fight mechanism).

## Mitigation Vector
This bias cannot be mitigated by presenting facts or debating. It is targeted and mitigated by:
- [Street Epistemology](../methodologies/street_epistemology.md) (which analyzes how a belief is held rather than what is held).
- [Socratic Questioning](../methodologies/socratic_questioning.md) (which allows the subject to discover internal contradictions independently).

Also, general countermeasures are detailed in the [Cognitive Bias Index](../cognitive_bias_and_fallacy_index.md).

## Safety and Ethics
Interventions must prioritize emotional containment and avoid confrontational validation loops to prevent triggering this effect. The protocols to manage this are defined in the [Ethical Guardrails and Harm Reduction Framework](../ethical_guardrails_and_harm_reduction_framework.md).
