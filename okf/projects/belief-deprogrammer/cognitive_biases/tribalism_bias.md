# Tribalism Bias

Tribalism Bias (or In-group Bias) is the primary force that reinforces belief structures within echo chambers and prevents individuals from considering outside perspectives.

## Operational Definition
A subject conforms to the dogma of their group (in-group) and reflexively dismisses, demonizes, or rationalizes away any information originating from outside the group (out-group).

## Psychological Origins
Evolutionary safety in numbers. Rejection by the tribe historically meant certain death, so the brain treats ideological conformity as a matter of survival.

## Mitigation Vector
This bias is mitigated by:
- [Street Epistemology](../methodologies/street_epistemology.md) (which avoids direct factual challenges and centers on personal epistemology).
- [Socratic Questioning](../methodologies/socratic_questioning.md) (allowing the subject to see the contradiction without making them feel like a traitor to the group).

## Safety and Ethics
Interventions must avoid attacking the group or its leaders directly, as this triggers the persecution complex and strengthens tribal boundaries. These rules are outlined in the [Ethical Guardrails and Harm Reduction Framework](../ethical_guardrails_and_harm_reduction_framework.md).
